Does the Swarm Model Generalize?
ODELIA · Swarm Learning · Validation Report

First successful 8-site, 20-round swarm training on real clinical data

Eight hospitals trained one model together for two days without a single dropout — the first time the full consortium has finished a 20-round run. On held-out data it outperforms the best model any single hospital could train alone. This report covers what the model achieves, and what made this attempt finish when earlier ones did not.

What “strict8” fixes

The swarm controller used to stop waiting once a minimum number of sites had answered the configuration step, then advance. Sites needing another second or two to bring up their job cell received the peer key-exchange before they could accept it — cannot forward req: no path — and were silently dropped. The run continued on a subset and still reported success. Requiring all eight to configure removes the race entirely.

Because the controller drops the slowest sites, and the slowest site is the one with the most data, the old default preferentially discarded the largest contributor.

The third change is procedural rather than technical: each fix was validated with a 1-round canary before committing to a multi-day run. That is why the fix sequence converged in a week after a long period of failures — the feedback loop went from ~30 hours to ~2.

The previous consortium ceiling

The remembered previous attempt involved seven sites and stopped at approximately round 11. It did not finish or produce a retained global checkpoint that can be scored on the challenge set.

This history is not independently recoverable from the available infrastructure: the server job store was re-provisioned on 20 July, and no matching seven-site job appears in the live-sync archive. Treat the site count and round as operator recollection until the original job identifier or meeting record is recovered. No external metric is assigned to that run.

Comparison caveat

The May figures were computed on 300 cases sample-weighted across institutions; these on the 165-exam test split. The comparison is indicative of direction, not a controlled head-to-head. Re-scoring the older checkpoints on this exact split would settle it, and is worth doing before any of these numbers are published.

What changed between May and now: two more sites, 20 rounds rather than a shorter run, and — from the May study's own “next steps” list — the global-best selection fix, so the swarm now publishes its best model instead of its last.

The predicted mix uses all three classes, but it is not the true mix: the model calls malignant 21 times where the truth is 37, and benign 34 times where the truth is 17. It is under-calling cancer and absorbing the difference into benign — the unsafe direction, not a conservative one. Note this coexists with a malignant-vs-rest AUROC of 0.887: the model ranks malignancy well, so what is mis-set is the decision threshold, not the discrimination. Threshold tuning on a calibration split should recover most of the missed cancers, and malignant recall — not accuracy — is the metric to report in any clinical framing.

Metrics do not reach the coordinator

Per-site validation is not synced centrally for peer-to-peer runs — the server's cross_val_results.json is empty, and no best-metric value appears anywhere in the job workspace. The 0.698 figure is carried over from the run hand-off notes and could not be re-derived from any surviving artefact — treat it as indicative only. The two site rows came from logging into individual hospitals. A published result cannot depend on that, and closing this gap is the highest-value engineering task before the next run.

An earlier version of this report was wrong

The first evaluation was run with the wrong model architecture — MST rather than the 1DivideAndConquer network this job actually trained (ResidualEncoderClsLightning). The prediction tool loaded the weights without complaint and produced plausible-looking but meaningless output: macro AUROC 0.43, near-constant predictions per centre, and never once the “benign” class.

That earlier output invited a compelling but false diagnosis — that the model had learned scanner signatures rather than anatomy. It had not. Re-run against the correct architecture, every metric roughly doubled and the class collapse disappeared. Anyone who saw the previous numbers should discard them.

Limits of this evaluation

  • Small samples. 165 exams pooled; 8–64 per centre. RUMC (n = 8) is indicative only.
  • Checkpoint choice matters. Best and final were both evaluated: the best checkpoint has slightly stronger AUROC, while the final checkpoint has higher accuracy and macro F1.
  • RSH labelling is still unconfirmed. Confirmation has been outstanding since July and is required before publication.
  • Baseline comparison is indicative. The single-site figures come from an earlier report on the same challenge set, but were not recomputed here under identical conditions.

What we recommend next

  • Roll out the new preprocessing to every partner. Put the version, validation gate and rollout schedule first on the next technical-meeting agenda.
  • Merge the reliability fixes. The changes that made this run finish are still carried as a draft pull request. Until they land, the next run depends on remembering to configure it by hand.
  • Fix central metric collection so per-site and cross-site validation reach the coordinator automatically.
  • Re-evaluate on the full 816-exam challenge set for the statistical power the test split alone cannot give.
  • Tune the decision threshold. The model ranks malignancy well (AUROC 0.887) but calls it only 21 times against 37 true cases. Fitting the operating point on a calibration split — and reporting malignant recall alongside accuracy — should recover most of those without retraining.
  • Investigate UMCU, the one centre where the model reads materially worse.
  • Complete the Duke evaluation. The 262-case held-out dataset is ready on dl0; evaluate both checkpoints after the active CI validation releases its GPU.
  • Recompute per-centre local baselines with identical preprocessing, cohort and evaluation code, to quantify what the swarm adds over local training centre by centre.
