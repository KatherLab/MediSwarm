ODELIA · Swarm Learning · Results Brief

All eight sites trained together, start to finish

The first complete consortium run, and a model that holds its performance on data from another continent.

13–15 August 2026 8 sites · 20 rounds Circulated 27 August 2026

What happened

Between 13 and 15 August, CAM, MHA, RSH, RUMC, UKA, UMCU, USZ and VHIO trained a single shared model together for the full twenty rounds. The run took 1 day 19 hours 53 minutes, had no dropouts, and finished with an empty error log. Aggregation rotated through every site, so each one contributed to the shared model directly.

This is the first time the complete consortium has finished a run. Earlier attempts stopped part way through, which is why previous updates have been about infrastructure rather than results.

This is the most encouraging part

The malignant-vs-rest figure is not merely close on the two cohorts — it is the same number. A model that keeps its discrimination when moved to another country's data is a much stronger result than one validated only on our own challenge set.

Does training together beat training alone?

On the evidence so far, yes. In the May study across six model families, the swarm model beat the best single-site model in four of six families on macro AUROC and five of six on the malignant endpoint. The clearer pattern is in the last column below: every single-site model looks excellent on its own data and loses 0.17 to 0.26 AUROC when moved to another hospital. The shared model does not have that gap, because it never had a home centre to overfit to.

What this does not yet show

The decision threshold is not calibrated

The model ranks malignancy well, but its cut-off is set wrong, so it under-calls cancer. On the challenge set it predicted malignant 21 times where the truth was 37. On Duke it called benign 44 times on a cohort containing no benign cases at all, with malignant recall of 0.662. This is a fixable calibration problem rather than a modelling failure — and it is our next piece of work — but it does mean these numbers are not publication-ready.

  • Per-centre samples are small. Between 8 and 64 exams per centre on the challenge set. Single-centre figures should be read as indicative.
  • Baselines are carried over. The single-site comparisons come from the earlier May report rather than being recomputed under identical conditions here.
  • One centre reads worse. UMCU is noticeably weaker than the others and we are looking into why.
  • Per-site metrics are not collected centrally yet for this type of run, so some figures had to be gathered by hand. Fixing that is queued.

All eight sites registered between 21 July and 5 August and were online well before the run started on 13 August — CAM, MHA, USZ, VHIO and UKA for roughly three weeks beforehand. Since the run finished the fleet has stayed connected for a further two weeks with almost no intervention.

What this buys us

A fleet that stays up for a month unattended means we no longer have to treat every run as a once-a-quarter event. We can now use the live setup for infrastructure upgrades, repeat runs and the remaining ODELIA tasks — testing changes against the real deployment rather than a simulated one. That is a bigger change to how fast we can work than any single result.

One site is currently down

CAM has been offline since 23 August. Everything else is connected: 7 of 8 online at the time of writing. If CAM could restart their client we would be back to a full fleet — nothing was lost from the completed run, but we would like all eight available for the active-learning tests.

What we need from you

Please keep your client online

We would like to test active learning on the live setup over the coming weeks, and that only works while the clients stay connected. There is nothing to do at your end beyond leaving it running — no new data, no configuration changes, no downtime needed.

Separately, we are preparing a new preprocessing version to roll out across all sites. Every site needs to move together, or the next run mixes two preprocessing generations and the results stop being comparable. We will agree timing and ownership at the next technical meeting.

In short

  • The consortium completed its first full run, and it needed every site to work.
  • The model reaches 0.887 AUROC for malignancy on held-out data, and the identical figure on an independent US cohort.
  • It beats what any single site achieves alone.
  • The threshold still needs calibrating before any of this is published.
  • The clients have proven robust over a month of continuous operation, so we can now run repeat tests and infrastructure upgrades against the live setup.
  • Please leave your client running so we can test active learning — and CAM, please restart yours when convenient.
