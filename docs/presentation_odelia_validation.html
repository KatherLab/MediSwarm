First successful 8-site, 20-round swarm training on real clinical data

Eight sites · twenty rounds · real patient data. Eight hospitals trained one model together for two days without a single dropout — the first time the full consortium has finished a 20-round run. On held-out data it outperforms the best model any single hospital could train alone.

Fix 1 of 2

What “strict8” fixes

A quorum default that silently trained on a subset
  • The controller stopped waiting once a minimum number of sites had answered the configuration step, then advanced.
  • A site needing another second or two to bring up its job cell received the peer key-exchange before it could accept it — cannot forward req: no path — and was silently dropped.
  • The run continued on whoever was left and still reported success.

The failure was biased, not random

The controller drops the slowest sites — and the slowest site is the one with the most data. The old default preferentially discarded the largest contributor. Requiring all eight to configure removes the race entirely.

And it reproduces the threshold problem exactly

The model called benign 44 times on a cohort with zero benign cases, and its malignant recall is only 0.662 despite an AUROC of 0.887. The discrimination travels; the operating point is wrong everywhere. This is now confirmed on two independent cohorts, which makes threshold calibration the clearest next win.

The right-hand column is the real argument

Every single-site model looks excellent on its own data — 0.78 to 0.91 — and loses 0.17 to 0.26 AUROC the moment it is moved to another hospital. The swarm model does not have that gap, because it never had a home centre to overfit to.

The previous ceiling

How far the consortium had got before

Seven sites, aborted around round 11
  • The best previous attempt was a seven-site run that aborted at roughly round 11 — the furthest the consortium had ever reached before this one.
  • It never produced an archived global model, so it cannot be scored on the challenge set. There is no external number for it to be compared against.
  • That is the honest baseline for this run: not a worse score, but no score at all. Every earlier attempt either stopped early or left nothing behind that could be evaluated.
  • This is also exactly why the metric-plumbing gap matters — a run that dies mid-way currently leaves us with almost nothing to learn from.

Provenance note

The server's job store was re-provisioned on 20 July and retains nothing before that date, and this run is not in the live-sync archive either. The round-11 figure is from operator recollection, not a recovered log — worth pinning down before it goes in a paper.

Honesty

Limits of this evaluation

  • Small samples per centre. 165 ODELIA exams, 8–64 per centre — RUMC (n = 8) is indicative only. The 260-case Duke cohort mitigates this for the malignant endpoint, but not per centre.
  • Checkpoint choice matters. Best and final were both evaluated: macro AUROC was 0.820 vs 0.818 and malignant AUROC 0.887 vs 0.873; final accuracy was higher, 0.782 vs 0.752.
  • RSH labelling is still unconfirmed. We have been waiting on data-labelling confirmation from RSH since July. Their contribution needs verification before any of this is published.
  • Baselines are indicative. Single-site figures come from an earlier report on the same challenge set, not recomputed here under identical conditions.

Metrics do not reach the coordinator

For peer-to-peer runs the server's cross_val_results.json is empty. The two internal figures we have came from logging into individual hospitals by hand. A published result cannot depend on that.

Future work

What comes next

  • Roll out the new preprocessing to every partner. Make the version, validation gate and rollout schedule the first decision at the next technical meeting.
  • Merge the reliability fixes. The changes that made this run finish are still carried as a draft pull request. Until they land, the next run depends on remembering to configure it by hand.
  • Fix central metric collection so per-site and cross-site validation reach the coordinator automatically. Highest-value engineering task before the next run.
  • Tune the decision threshold on a calibration split, and report malignant recall alongside accuracy.
  • Re-evaluate on the full 816-exam challenge set for statistical power the test split alone cannot give.
  • Score the final-round checkpoint on Duke as well, to confirm the two checkpoints agree on the independent cohort too.
  • Investigate UMCU, the one centre where the model reads materially worse.
  • Recompute per-centre local baselines with the same preprocessing, cohort and evaluation code, to quantify what the swarm adds centre by centre.
