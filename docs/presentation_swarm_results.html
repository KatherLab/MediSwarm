The problem and the question

  • Medical imaging is siloed. Breast-MRI lives behind hospital governance — data cannot be pooled centrally.
  • Swarm / federated learning trains one shared model by exchanging model weights, never images.
  • The catch: each site sees a different population — scanners, prevalence, demographics all shift.
  • Central question: does aggregating across sites beat the best single-site model on unseen data?

What we evaluated

One task, two endpoints, three studies
  • Task: 3-class lesion grade per breast — 0 none, 1 benign, 2 malignant (unilateral MRI).
  • Sites: CAM, MHA, RSH, RUMC, UKA, UMCU (+ USZ onboarding).
  • Two endpoints: internal validation (own site) vs external ODELIA challenge test (held-out).
  • Study A — swarm mechanism proof (Duke, 3 clients).
  • Study B — 6 models × 6 sites swarm, swarm-vs-single-site on external data.
  • Study C — single-site checkpoint transfer to the external challenge.

Study A — what the first swarm taught us

Three issues that became guardrails for Study B
  • Label coverage: class 1 (benign) absent ⇒ 3-class macro AUROC undefined — coverage must be checked first.
  • Best-model selection broke: aggregator looked for accuracy but clients logged val/ACC ⇒ run emitted the last model, not the global-best.
  • Split hygiene: logs flagged UID overlap across train / val / test ⇒ needs split auditing before numbers are trusted.
  • All three are data / plumbing issues — not modeling issues.

Study B — why: aggregation regularizes against site shift

The best single-site model overfits its own population
  • Best site-local models score 0.78–0.91 internally … but drop to 0.57–0.73 externally.
  • Swarm models score lower internally yet higher externally — classic generalization trade.
  • Aggregating across heterogeneous sites acts as regularization against distribution shift.

Study C — internal scores do not predict transfer

High internal validation ≠ good external generalization
  • CAM 1DC: internal Class-2 AUROC 0.965 → external 0.682.
  • RUMC MST: internal accuracy 0.995 → external macro 0.495 (val split was ~199:0:1 — degenerate).
  • RSH 5Pimed: external Class-2 AUROC 0.456 but recall 0.879 — predicts ‘malignant’ indiscriminately.
  • Lesson: select on representative/external validation; under imbalance, trust AUROC + recall, not accuracy.
External challenge macro AUROC by checkpoint — ranking differs sharply from internal scores.

Cross-cutting: data quality is the real bottleneck

Every study surfaced a data/plumbing issue before a modeling one
  • Label coverage — missing benign class breaks 3-class metrics (Study A).
  • Split hygiene — UID overlap across train/val/test inflates scores (Study A).
  • Metric-name mismatch silently disabled global-best selection (Study A & 5Pimed in B).
  • Class imbalance — accuracy is misleading; degenerate single-class val splits (RUMC, USZ 9% malignant).
  • ⇒ Plausibility auditing is now a gate, not an afterthought.

Takeaways

  • 1. The swarm pipeline runs end-to-end: 6 models × 6 sites, complete artifacts.
  • 2. Swarm global generalizes better than the best single-site model externally (4/6 win, 1 tie). Core result.
  • 3. Internal validation overstates performance — the external challenge is the honest endpoint.
  • 4. 1DivideAndConquer and 4LME_ABMIL are the strongest families (ext. AUROC up to 0.86).
  • 5. Data audits catch most failures before modeling.
